# Tastylive (Tom Sosnoff) Most Used Option Strategies

Source: shared by Mike from a tastylive video summary, 2026-08-02. These are described as the boring but effective strategies used by professional retail traders per this source.

Saved for future use: when Mikes scanning system identifies candidate trades, cross check the candidate against these parameters before presenting it as genuinely aligned with tastylive methodology.

## Strategies To Get Long

### 1. Short Puts
Simplest and most capital efficient strategy. Sell OTM puts 35 to 50 DTE. Target 16 to 22 deltas at the expected move, buying power reduction of 20 percent of strike price. Best for beaten down stocks with IVR 30 percent or more. Profit target 50 percent of premium collected.

### 2. Jade Lizard
Short put combined with short call spread kicker. Position so there is no upside risk, achieved by ensuring total premium collected exceeds the width of the strikes on the short call spread. Approximately 40 DTE, sell OTM put and call spread just inside expected move. Profit target approximately 50 percent of total premium.

### 3. Covered Calls
Classic basis reduction trade. Execute as a single trade, stock and options together, on stocks with low price and high implied volatility. 40 to 60 DTE, short strike between 25 and 30 delta. Consider taking profits if short strike is breached, always close as a single trade.

### 4. Short Put Spreads
Defined risk alternative for expensive stocks. Target credit between 30 and 35 percent of width of strikes. Profit target 50 percent of credit received.

### 5. Put Ratio Spreads
High probability strategy for oversold stocks with high IV. Use a 1x2 approach: long strike at expected move, sell twice as many further OTM puts to generate a net credit. Profit target at least the net credit received.

## Strategies To Get Short

### 1. Short Call Spreads
Capital efficient way to manage long deltas. 30 to 50 DTE, sell just inside the expected move. Collect between 30 and 40 percent of width of strikes. Profit target around 50 percent.

### 2. Broken Wing Butterfly (BWB)
Skip strike butterfly, long 1, short 2, long 1. Offers protection against up moves. Approximately one month duration, execute for a net credit to increase probability of profit. Long strike at expected move, short strike just outside it. Profit target at least the net credit.

### 3. Unbalanced/Skewed Iron Condor
Short premium trade with short deltas. Sell a call spread that is wider than the put spread, for example 10 dollar wide call vs 5 dollar wide put. Approximately 40 DTE, short strikes at expected move, collect roughly 50 percent of width of the narrower side strikes. Profit target 50 percent of total credit.

## Strategies To Sell Volatility And Premium

### 1. Iron Condor
Defined risk version of a short strangle. Target stocks with super high IVR expected to remain rangebound. Combined credit of 30 to 40 percent of width of strikes. Profit target 30 to 50 percent of credit received.

### 2. Short Strangles
Described by this source as the number one and best performing strategy, accounting for a massive portion of profits. Use 16 to 20 delta short strikes at the expected move with 45 DTE. Requires super high IVR, traders must keep size in check and ensure underlyings are non correlated. Profit target 50 percent of credit received.
Note: this closely matches Mikes own stated approach (delta neutral, theta harvesting, short premium, similar DTE and delta range).

### 3. Price Reversion To The Mean
Directional trading based on the principle that expanded volatility must eventually contract. Methods: selling volatility directly, or Pairs Trading (Basis ARB). For pairs, use futures for capital efficiency and understand volatility adjusted notional to determine the correct ratio. Profit target subjective, should align with risk or percentage of daily move.
